Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript "Context-Aware Adaptive Perception for
Motorcycle-Dominant Mixed Traffic: Class-Conditioned Motion Modeling and
Real-Time Feasibility on Commodity CPUs" for consideration as an original
research paper in the Journal of Real-Time Image Processing.

Context and importance. In motorcycle-dominant cities such as Ho Chi Minh
City, traffic cameras are increasingly used for incident alerting and adaptive
signal control, and both depend on a detector-plus-tracker front end that must
run in real time on the CPUs of municipal video servers. Standard trackers
model every object with one linear motion model, which fragments the identities
of weaving motorcycles. We introduce a class-conditioned constant-turn-rate
extended Kalman filter for motorcycles, with uncertainty-gated re-association
and confidence-scaled measurement noise. On 600 human-annotated rush-hour
frames it raises motorcycle IDF1 from 0.858 to 0.907 and removes 80% of
identity switches relative to ByteTrack on identical detections, and it also
leads OC-SORT and BoT-SORT run on the same detections, both with their
published settings and with matched detection thresholds. We also
report two components that did not help on our data as characterized negative
results.

Real-time issues addressed. Beyond reporting speed, the manuscript treats
real-time operation as a design constraint:
1. End-to-end CPU latency with the detector included, broken down by stage
   (decode, context estimation, detection, tracking, scoring), against the
   40 ms budget implied by the 25 fps source, with 95th percentiles.
2. Complexity and memory of every proposed module (constant-size 5-state
   filter per track, about 1 kB of state per track) and their measured cost:
   the motion model adds 0.85 ms per frame to the tracker, 2.8% of a frame.
3. The finding that the detector, at 91% of the frame time, decides real-time
   feasibility on a CPU, and the design choice that meets the budget: a
   detection head instead of a segmentation head (2.25x faster) at 448 px
   input reaches 26.6 ms per frame (37.6 fps).
4. A comparison of inference runtimes on the same CPU (OpenVINO, ONNX Runtime,
   half precision), all slower than PyTorch with default export settings.
5. A measurement protocol (one configuration per process, discarded warm-up)
   with the size of the errors it prevents (a 3.2x spread between identical
   runs timed in one process).
