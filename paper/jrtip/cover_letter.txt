Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript "Context-Aware Adaptive Perception for
Motorcycle-Dominant Mixed Traffic: Class-Conditioned Motion Modeling and
Real-Time Feasibility on Commodity CPUs" for consideration as an original
research paper in the Journal of Real-Time Image Processing.

Context and importance. In motorcycle-dominant cities such as Ho Chi Minh
City, traffic cameras are increasingly used for incident alerting and adaptive
signal control, and both depend on a detector-plus-tracker front end that must
run in real time on the CPUs of municipal video servers. We evaluate a
context-aware perception pipeline for this setting: a controller that sets the
tracker's operating point from the traffic context, a class-conditioned
constant-turn-rate motion model for motorcycles, and a track reliability score
with separate thresholds for alerting and signal control. We report every
component as measured on three human-annotated segments of one camera, under
one development protocol with identical cached detections. The motorcycle
motion model cuts identity switches in dense traffic by 38-56% against
ByteTrack, with a seed-corrected IDF1 gain below 0.02, and OC-SORT with matched
thresholds tracks better still; the reliability score filters nothing at its
threshold, and [the context controller's effect after recalibration]. Two
evaluation findings reach beyond this pipeline: ground truth corrected from one
tracker's pre-labels favours that tracker by about 0.04 IDF1, which a blind
audit quantifies and a bracket corrects; and the real-time operating point
costs accuracy through small objects.

Real-time issues addressed. Beyond reporting speed, the manuscript treats
real-time operation as a design constraint:
1. The accuracy cost of real-time detector operating points, stratified by
   object size: at 640 px input the detector misses 99% of the motorcycles
   under 20 px tall, against 10% at 1,280 px, and 69-76% of the annotated
   motorcycles are under 40 px.
2. End-to-end CPU latency with the detector included, at four input sizes
   (448 to 1,280 px), broken down by stage, against the 40 ms budget of the
   25 fps source. [Insert the measured values after the latency run.]
3. Complexity and memory of every proposed module (a constant-size 5-state
   filter, about 1 kB of state per motorcycle track).
4. Design choices measured on the same CPU: a detection head instead of a
   segmentation head (2.25x faster at 640 px), and inference runtimes
   (OpenVINO, ONNX Runtime, half precision), all slower than PyTorch with
   default export settings.
5. A measurement protocol (one configuration per process, discarded warm-up,
   verified processor performance state) with the size of the errors it
   prevents: a 3.2x spread between identical runs timed in one process, and
   throttled runs at 32-64% of nominal processor performance, which we
   discarded.
