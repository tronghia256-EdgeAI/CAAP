\begin{abstract}
Traffic-surveillance pipelines pair a one-stage detector with a tracker that
applies one linear motion model and fixed thresholds to every object, and
motorcycle-dominant mixed traffic strains both choices. We present a
perception pipeline with three components: (C1) a context
estimator and hysteresis-stabilized controller that set the tracker's operating
point online; (C2) a class-conditioned motion model that tracks motorcycles
with a constant-turn-rate-and-velocity extended Kalman filter inside ByteTrack,
with covariance gating for lost tracks and confidence-scaled measurement noise;
and (C3) a track reliability score with separate thresholds for incident
alerting and signal-control observations. On three human-annotated segments of
Ho Chi Minh City CCTV, at the 448-pixel real-time input and on identical
detections, C2 cuts motorcycle identity switches in dense traffic by 56\%
against ByteTrack, through its CTRV model alone, while its seed-corrected IDF1 gain is at most 0.02; OC-SORT with matched thresholds tracks
better on both counts. C3 filters nothing here but keeps a real accident alert in
a one-clip case study, and C1, recalibrated, switches regimes as intended
without improving tracking. Two findings generalize. Ground
truth corrected from one tracker's pre-labels favours that tracker by about
0.04 IDF1 through the boxes it contains rather than their geometry; a blind
audit finds 80\% of the other tracker's unmatched runs real,
and we bracket the bias. And the real-time operating point costs accuracy
where motorcycles are small: at the 448-pixel input the detector misses virtually all of
those under 20 pixels tall (99\% at 640 pixels), and a region-of-interest mask that truncates far
vehicles adds to the loss.
\keywords{Multi-object tracking \and Extended Kalman filter \and Mixed traffic
\and Motorcycles \and Annotation bias \and Real-time video analytics}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
%% =====================================================================

Road traffic crashes killed an estimated 1.19 million people in 2021; 92\% of
these deaths occur in low- and middle-income countries, and riders of powered
two- and three-wheelers account for 30\% of them~\cite{WHO2023_roadsafety}. In
Viet Nam, motorized two- and three-wheelers made up 93\% of the 50.7 million
vehicles registered in 2016~\cite{WHO2018_roadsafety}. Urban intersections are
increasingly covered by fixed CCTV cameras, and two applications motivate
turning that footage into structured perception in real time: incident
alerting, which must not flood operators with false alarms, and adaptive signal
control by multi-agent reinforcement learning~\cite{Chu2020_marltsc,Yu2022_mappo},
which needs complete and temporally consistent observations of the approaching
vehicles. Both consume the output of the same front end: a one-stage
detector~\cite{Redmon2016_yolo,Jocher2024_yolo11} followed by a
tracking-by-detection tracker~\cite{Bewley2016_sort,Zhang2022_bytetrack}.

In motorcycle-dominant mixed traffic this front end fails in two ways. The
first is \emph{identity fragmentation}: motorcycles weave through the gaps
between larger vehicles and occlude one another, and the constant-velocity
Kalman filter shared by mainstream trackers coasts along a straight line while
the hidden motorcycle keeps turning, so the reappearing detection falls outside
the association region and receives a new identity. To a signal controller, a
fragmented identity is one vehicle that vanishes and another that appears from
nowhere. The second is \emph{operating-point rigidity}: thresholds and track
lifetimes are tuned once, for one regime, although density, illumination and the
share of spurious tracks change over the day.

Recent trackers strengthen association with appearance
cues~\cite{Wojke2017_deepsort,Du2023_strongsort}, observation-centric
corrections~\cite{Cao2023_ocsort} or learned motion
predictors~\cite{Qin2023_motiontrack,Lv2024_diffmot}, but they model every object
class with the same kind of state, and the benchmarks that drive them---crowds
of pedestrians~\cite{Dendorfer2020_mot20}, dancers and
athletes~\cite{Sun2022_dancetrack,Cui2023_sportsmot}, or road traffic without a
motorcycle class~\cite{Wen2020_uadetrac}---do not exhibit motorcycle weaving.
Kinematic models with an explicit turn rate, such as constant turn rate and
velocity (CTRV), are standard in automotive state
estimation~\cite{Schubert2008_ctrv,BarShalom2001_estimation} but rare in
image-plane tracking-by-detection and, to our knowledge, have not been selected
by the detected object class. This paper studies a pipeline that adapts along
two axes, per object class through the motion model and per traffic regime
through the operating point. It contributes three components and two
evaluation results:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{C1}, a context estimator and adaptive controller that select the
tracker's confirmation threshold, lost-track buffer and motorcycle motion model
online, with smoothing and hysteresis (Sect.~\ref{sec:c1});
\item \textbf{C2}, a class-conditioned motion model: motorcycles are predicted by
a CTRV extended Kalman filter (EKF) inside ByteTrack~\cite{Zhang2022_bytetrack},
whose innovation covariance re-opens association for lost tracks and whose
measurement noise is scaled by detection confidence; we derive the filter, bound
its discretization error and characterize its observability (Sect.~\ref{sec:c2});
\item \textbf{C3}, one reliability score per track, split by two thresholds into a
precision-first alerting branch and a recall-first observation branch
(Sect.~\ref{sec:c3});
\item a measurement of seed bias in pre-labelled tracking ground truth, with a
blind audit and a bracketed correction (Sect.~\ref{subsec:seedbias});
\item the accuracy cost of real-time front-end choices, input size and
region-of-interest masking, measured by object size (Sect.~\ref{subsec:operating}).
\end{itemize}

We evaluate every configuration under one development protocol: three
600-frame segments of one camera, ground truth reconciled after a blind audit,
a region of interest, and identical cached detections at four input sizes. At
the 448-px real-time input, C2 reduces identity switches in dense traffic by
56\% against ByteTrack (38\% at 640~px), entirely through its CTRV model, while
its IDF1 gain is at most 0.02 after correcting for seed bias; OC-SORT~\cite{Cao2023_ocsort} with matched
thresholds is stronger on both counts, and C2's motion model does not improve
it. C3 filters nothing at its threshold, and C1, recalibrated, switches regimes
as intended without improving tracking.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
%% =====================================================================

\subsection{Tracking by detection and motion models}\label{subsec:rw_mot}

SORT~\cite{Bewley2016_sort} combined a constant-velocity Kalman filter with IoU
matching; DeepSORT~\cite{Wojke2017_deepsort} added appearance descriptors and a
Mahalanobis gate under the filter's covariance; ByteTrack~\cite{Zhang2022_bytetrack}
recovered occluded objects by associating low-score detections in a second
stage. OC-SORT~\cite{Cao2023_ocsort} re-updates the filter after an occlusion;
BoT-SORT~\cite{Aharon2022_botsort} and UCMCTrack~\cite{Yi2024_ucmctrack}
compensate camera motion; StrongSORT~\cite{Du2023_strongsort} adopts the
confidence-dependent measurement noise of GIAOTracker~\cite{Du2021_giaotracker};
Hybrid-SORT~\cite{Yang2024_hybridsort} adds confidence and height cues to the
state; and FastTracker~\cite{Hashempoor2025_fasttracker} targets vehicles with
occlusion-aware re-identification, road-structure priors and a class-aware
Kalman filter whose velocity and acceleration bounds depend on the class. All of
these represent every object with positions and velocities and no turn rate.
Learned predictors such as MotionTrack~\cite{Qin2023_motiontrack} and
DiffMOT~\cite{Lv2024_diffmot} replace the Kalman prediction with networks that
need training trajectories from the target domain, which for motorcycle traffic
are not public. Model-based alternatives come from vehicle state estimation:
Schubert et al.~\cite{Schubert2008_ctrv} found that curvilinear models with an
explicit turn rate, such as CTRV, track maneuvering vehicles better than linear
ones, interacting-multiple-model filters~\cite{Blom1988_imm,BarShalom2001_estimation}
switch between such models, and in image-plane tracking K{\i}ratl{\i} and
Ero{\u{g}}lu~\cite{Kiratli2025_yolo11efac} pair DeepSORT with an EKF for nonlinear
motion in UAV video. C2 selects a CTRV model by class and uses its
covariance only to add matches for lost tracks (Table~\ref{tab:positioning}).

\subsection{Perception in mixed traffic}\label{subsec:rw_mixed}

UA-DETRAC~\cite{Wen2020_uadetrac} provides fixed-camera sequences with identity
annotations but no motorcycle class, and D$^2$-City~\cite{Che2019_d2city}
annotates tracks of twelve classes in dashcam video. Two-wheelers appear mostly
in datasets built for other tasks, such as real-time helmet detection for
two-wheeler riders~\cite{An2026_rspvyolo}. Closest to our setting, Vu et
al.~\cite{Vu2025_counting} detect, track and count vehicles at
motorcycle-dominated intersections in Viet Nam; their evaluation targets count
accuracy rather than identity preservation. TrafficMOT~\cite{Liu2024_trafficmot}
targets multi-class tracking in complex traffic, and the benchmark released with
FastTracker~\cite{Hashempoor2025_fasttracker} provides twelve urban CCTV
sequences with nine traffic classes, motorcycles among them, without reporting
their location or the share of two-wheelers. We found no public fixed-camera
benchmark documented as motorcycle-dominant with identity annotations over
sequences long enough to test re-association across occlusions, and therefore
annotated our own (Sect.~\ref{subsec:data}).

\subsection{Adaptive perception and downstream tasks}\label{subsec:rw_adaptive}

Adaptive perception mostly targets appearance shift, for example through
image enhancement learned for adverse weather~\cite{Liu2022_iayolo} or
domain-adaptive detection~\cite{Chen2018_dafrcnn}. Within tracking, adaptation is local to one
detection or track: confidence-scaled measurement
noise~\cite{Du2021_giaotracker,Du2023_strongsort} or confidence-aware
association~\cite{Yang2024_hybridsort,Liu2024_cotracker}.
COTracker~\cite{Liu2024_cotracker} argues that fixed confidence thresholds make
multi-stage association unstable across scenarios and removes them; C1 instead
keeps the thresholds and chooses them from measured scene context. Vision-based
accident detection has been surveyed recently~\cite{Fang2024_accidentsurvey};
datasets such as CADP~\cite{Shah2018_cadp}, DoTA~\cite{Yao2023_dota} and that of
Bao et al.~\cite{Bao2020_ccd} support clip-level detection and anticipation with
operating points fixed offline. How one track stream can serve both an alerting
channel and a controller with opposite precision--recall preferences has
received little attention.

\subsection{Real-time inference on CPUs}\label{subsec:rw_realtime}

Recent real-time detectors such as YOLO11~\cite{Jocher2024_yolo11},
YOLOv10~\cite{Wang2024_yolov10} and RT-DETR~\cite{Zhao2024_rtdetr} trade accuracy
against latency, and lightweight detectors for traffic participants compress the
network further, e.g., by feature distillation~\cite{An2026_tpdyolo}. Deployment
commonly exports the network to ONNX Runtime~\cite{ONNXRuntime} or
OpenVINO~\cite{OpenVINO}, optionally with integer
quantization~\cite{Jacob2018_quant}. Real-time claims are usually made on GPUs,
whereas many municipal video servers have only CPUs; we therefore report CPU
latency with the detector included.

\subsection{System overview}\label{subsec:overview}

At frame $t$ the context estimator (C1) summarizes the traffic state from the
frame and from the tracks produced at $t-1$, and the controller maps it to the
tracker's operating point (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). The detector runs at a
fixed confidence floor of 0.1, so that low-score boxes remain available to
ByteTrack's second stage; the adaptive threshold acts on the tracker, not on the
detector. In the modified ByteTrack, motorcycle tracks are predicted by a
CTRV-EKF and all other classes by the original linear filter (C2). Every output
track receives a reliability score (C3), and two thresholds route tracks to the
accident branch and to the observation builder of a signal controller. A
detection is a box $\mathbf{b}=[x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2]$ with confidence $s\in[0,1]$ and
class $\kappa\in\{\textsc{car},\textsc{motorcycle},\textsc{bus},\textsc{truck},\textsc{accident}\}$.

\subsection{C1: Context estimator and adaptive controller}\label{sec:c1}

At frame $t$ the estimator computes the state
$\mathcal{S}_t=(\rho_t,h_t,\beta_t,\phi_t)$: the number of tracks output at $t-1$
(density), the hour of the recording, the mean grayscale intensity (illumination
proxy) and the fraction of tracks younger than three frames (a false-positive
proxy). Density and brightness are smoothed by an exponential moving average
$\tilde{x}_t=\alpha x_t+(1-\alpha)\tilde{x}_{t-1}$ with $\alpha=0.1$. The
controller assigns one of three conditions, with priority \textsc{night} $>$
\textsc{dense-peak} $>$ \textsc{default}: \textsc{night} if $\tilde\beta_t<80$,
\textsc{dense-peak} if $\tilde\rho_t\ge\rho^{*}$ and $h_t\in[7,20]$, where
$\rho^{*}$ is the 75th percentile of $\tilde\rho_t$ over the frames outside the
annotated segments, fixed per detector and input size before any C1 result is
scored. Each condition
sets $\Theta_t=(c_{\mathrm{trk}},L_{\mathrm{buf}},\mathcal{M})$: the confirmation
threshold that replaces ByteTrack's high/low split (new tracks need
$s\ge c_{\mathrm{trk}}+0.1$), the lost-track buffer in frames, and the filter a
new motorcycle track receives and keeps for its lifetime. \textsc{dense-peak}
uses $(0.50,30,\text{CTRV})$, calibrated on the dense segment under our earlier
protocol (originally $(0.35,45)$); \textsc{night} $(0.55,20,\text{linear})$ is
never exercised by our data; \textsc{default} is $(0.45,30,\text{linear})$. The false-positive proxy is
exposed but not used by the rules. A new condition takes effect only after
$N_h=15$ consecutive frames (0.6~s).

\subsection{C2: Class-conditioned CTRV motion model}\label{sec:c2}

ByteTrack's filter assumes constant velocity in the image plane. This suits
cars, which change heading slowly, but a weaving motorcycle turns continuously,
so during an occlusion the linear filter coasts along a tangent while the
vehicle follows an arc. Motorcycle tracks therefore receive a CTRV EKF; other
classes keep ByteTrack's linear filter behind the same interface.

\paragraph{Process model.}
The state $\mathbf{x}=[p_x,p_y,v,\theta,\omega]^{\top}$ holds the box centre
(px), speed (px/frame), heading and turn rate (rad/frame). With $\Delta t$ one
frame,
\begin{equation}
f(\mathbf{x})=\bigl[p_x+v\cos\theta\,\Delta t,\;p_y+v\sin\theta\,\Delta t,\;
v,\;\theta+\omega\Delta t,\;\omega\bigr]^{\top},\label{eq:ctrv}
\end{equation}
with additive noise $\mathbf{Q}=\mathrm{diag}(0.5,0.5,0.1,10^{-3},10^{-4})$, so a
lost track coasts along an arc. The heading is wrapped to $[-\pi,\pi)$. The
Jacobian is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F}=\begin{bmatrix}
1&0&c\Delta t&-vs\Delta t&0\\
0&1&s\Delta t&vc\Delta t&0\\
0&0&1&0&0\\
0&0&0&1&\Delta t\\
0&0&0&0&1
\end{bmatrix},\quad c=\cos\theta,\ s=\sin\theta.\label{eq:jacobian}
\end{equation}
We use this forward-Euler form instead of the exact CTRV integral because it has
no singularity at $\omega\to0$. Over one step the exact displacement is a chord
of length $(2v/\omega)\sin(\omega\Delta t/2)$ at angle $\theta+\omega\Delta t/2$;
to first order the two differ by $\tfrac12 v|\omega|\Delta t^{2}$, i.e., 0.5~px at
10~px/frame and 0.1~rad/frame, well below the measurement noise
($\sqrt{10}\approx3.2$~px).

\paragraph{Filtering.}
Prediction is $\hat{\mathbf{x}}^-=f(\hat{\mathbf{x}})$,
$\mathbf{P}^-=\mathbf{F}\mathbf{P}\mathbf{F}^{\top}+\mathbf{Q}$. The measurement is
the detection centre, $\mathbf{H}=[\mathbf{I}_2\;\mathbf{0}_{2\times3}]$, so the
update is a standard Kalman step with gain
$\mathbf{K}=\mathbf{P}^-\mathbf{H}^{\top}\mathbf{S}^{-1}$,
$\mathbf{S}=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{P}^-\mathbf{H}^{\top}+\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{eff}}$, and
the Joseph form
$\mathbf{P}=(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{K}\mathbf{H})\mathbf{P}^-(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{K}\mathbf{H})^{\top}+\mathbf{K}\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{eff}}\mathbf{K}^{\top}$
keeps $\mathbf{P}$ symmetric and positive definite~\cite{BarShalom2001_estimation}.
The base noise is $\mathbf{R}=\mathrm{diag}(10,10)$~px$^2$. Box size is taken from
the last matched detection. A track starts at $[c_x,c_y,0,0,0]^{\top}$ with
$\mathbf{P}_0=\mathrm{diag}(10,10,100,\pi^2,1)$; because speed and heading are not
observable from one detection (Proposition~\ref{prop:obs}), the first update sets
$v$ and $\theta$ from the displacement between the first two detections (if
above 1~px) and resets the corresponding rows and columns of $\mathbf{P}$
(variances 10 and 0.5). Clearing whole rows and columns matters: stale
cross-covariances would otherwise break positive definiteness.

\begin{proposition}[Local observability]\label{prop:obs}
Linearize~\eqref{eq:ctrv} at speed $v$ and heading $\theta$ and measure positions
only. The observability matrix
$\mathcal{O}=[\mathbf{H};\mathbf{H}\mathbf{F};\mathbf{H}\mathbf{F}^{2}]$ has full
rank 5 if and only if $v\neq0$; for $v=0$ its rank is 3, and heading and turn
rate are unobservable.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
From~\eqref{eq:jacobian}, for $k=1,2$,
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{F}^{k}=\begin{bmatrix}
1&0&kc\Delta t&-kvs\Delta t&-\binom{k}{2}vs\Delta t^{2}\\
0&1&ks\Delta t&kvc\Delta t&\binom{k}{2}vc\Delta t^{2}
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
Subtracting the rows of $\mathbf{H}$, and then twice the second block from the
third, leaves for the columns $(v,\theta,\omega)$ a $2\times2$ block with
determinant $v\Delta t^{2}$ and an $\omega$ column of norm $|v|\Delta t^{2}$.
Both vanish if and only if $v=0$, in which case the $\theta$ and $\omega$
columns are zero.\qed
\end{proof}

Hence at least three detections are needed before the turn rate is constrained,
so its estimate lags a change of turning direction; and a motorcycle waiting at a
stop line has unobservable heading and turn rate, which is why the bootstrap
waits for a displacement above the noise floor. No training data are needed.

\paragraph{Uncertainty-gated re-association.}
After a long occlusion the predicted and reappearing boxes may not overlap at
all, so IoU matching cannot recover the track at any threshold. For each
\emph{lost} motorcycle track we compute, for every detection centre $\mathbf{z}$
of the same class, $d^{2}=(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{H}\hat{\mathbf{x}})^{\top}\mathbf{S}^{-1}(\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{H}\hat{\mathbf{x}})$
with the base $\mathbf{R}$. Pairs with $d^{2}<\chi^{2}_{2,0.95}=5.991$ enter the
first-stage cost matrix at
\begin{equation}
c=\min\bigl(1-\mathrm{IoU},\;c_0+(1-c_0)\,d^{2}/\chi^{2}_{2,0.95}\bigr),\quad c_0=0.5,\label{eq:gatecost}
\end{equation}
so a rescued pair never costs less than an overlap match with IoU 0.5: the gate
only makes a match \emph{possible} where overlap offers none. ByteTrack fuses
every first-stage cost with the detection score, $\tilde c=1-(1-c)s$, and rejects
$\tilde c>m=0.8$; substituting~\eqref{eq:gatecost}, a gate-only pair survives iff
\begin{equation}
d^{2}\le\chi^{2}_{2,0.95}\bigl(1-0.4/s\bigr).\label{eq:admission}
\end{equation}
Since first-stage detections have $s\ge0.5$, the effective region ranges from
the 45\% ellipse ($d^{2}\le1.20$) for the weakest admissible detection to the
83\% ellipse ($d^{2}\le3.59$) for a fully confident one: the gate is implicitly
confidence-weighted.

\paragraph{Confidence-scaled noise and interaction.}
Following the noise-scale-adaptive (NSA) filter~\cite{Du2021_giaotracker,Du2023_strongsort},
the update uses $\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mathbf{R}\cdot\mathrm{clip}(1-s,0.05,1)$:
confident detections pull the state onto the measurement. The gate and NSA act
on the same quantities. With the base $\mathbf{R}$ the filter smooths heavily, so
on a weaving motorcycle the predicted centre lags while $\mathbf{P}$, and with it
$\mathbf{S}$, stays large; the admission ellipse is then wide and mis-centred,
and the detection it admits after an occlusion is often a neighbouring
motorcycle's. NSA reduces both the lag and the posterior covariance, so the same
gate admits the right detection. We therefore treat C2 as one mechanism
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:c2}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{C2 for one frame}\label{alg:c2}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require tracks $\mathcal{T}$; detections $\mathcal{D}_h$ ($s\ge c_{\mathrm{trk}}$), $\mathcal{D}_l$
\For{$\tau\in\mathcal{T}$}
  \State predict with CTRV-EKF if $\tau$ is a motorcycle, else linear KF
\EndFor
\State $C_{\tau j}\gets1-\mathrm{IoU}(\hat{\mathbf{b}}_\tau,\mathbf{b}_j)$, $\tau\in\mathcal{T}$, $j\in\mathcal{D}_h$
\For{lost CTRV $\tau$, $j\in\mathcal{D}_h$ with $\kappa_j=\kappa_\tau$, $d^2<\chi^2_{2,0.95}$}
  \State $C_{\tau j}\gets\min\bigl(C_{\tau j},\,c_0+(1-c_0)d^{2}/\chi^{2}_{2,0.95}\bigr)$
\EndFor
\State associate on $1-(1-C_{\tau j})s_j$ with threshold $m$; then $\mathcal{D}_l$
\State update CTRV tracks with $\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{eff}}$, others with the linear KF
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{C3: Dual-task track reliability score}\label{sec:c3}

Accident alerting must be precise, because every alert interrupts an operator;
the observation builder of a signal controller must be complete, because a
dropped vehicle is a hole in the controller's state. C3 computes one score per
track over the last $N=10$ frames,
\begin{equation}
S(\tau)=0.10A+0.20C+0.25M+0.15K+0.30D,\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
with normalized age $A=\min(\mathrm{age}/30,1)$, detection consistency $C$
(fraction of frames with a match), smoothness $M$ (mean IoU between predicted
and matched box), class stability $K$ and mean detection confidence $D$. The
confidence term separates ghost tracks that ByteTrack's second stage sustains on
low-score detections of shadows; with these design weights a maximally plausible
ghost (perfect $A$, $C$ and $K$, $M=0.7$, $D=0.2$) scores 0.685. With fewer than three samples the history terms return 0.5,
which keeps new tracks below the alerting threshold. The accident branch
consumes tracks with $S\ge\tau_{\mathrm{acc}}=0.70$ and the observation builder of
a MAPPO controller~\cite{Yu2022_mappo} tracks with $S\ge\tau_{\mathrm{obs}}=0.40$.
A frame raises an incident alert when a confirmed track of class
\textsc{accident} passes $\tau_{\mathrm{acc}}$ (tested in Sect.~\ref{subsec:c3-results}).

\subsection{Footage and ground truth}\label{subsec:data}

We use a fixed traffic camera at the Nam Ky Khoi Nghia--Vo Thi Sau intersection
in Ho Chi Minh City, recorded at 08:00 on a weekday in July 2017 at
$800\times450$ pixels and 25~fps, with motorcycle-dominant rush-hour traffic. The
stream decodes to 13,726 frames (9.2~min; the container header reports 22,876).
The recordings were collected by the authors.
From the track-count profile of the clip we annotated three 600-frame (24~s)
segments: \emph{freeflow} (frames 6,400--6,999, about 12 tracks per frame),
\emph{mixed} (2,500--3,099, about 26, straddling C1's density threshold) and
\emph{dense} (10,700--11,299, about 47). All three come from the camera on which
the method was developed, and the first two lie in frames 0--8,999, where proxy
metrics guided design decisions (Sect.~\ref{subsec:proxy}); the results are
therefore development results, and no independent camera was annotated.

%% Sources: docs/GT_COMPLETION_20261012.md, docs/GT_AUDIT_RECONCILIATION.md,
%% docs/GT_RECONCILED_20261013.md (final exports and receipt).
Annotation follows one convention in CVAT~\cite{CVAT}: a motorcycle box covers
vehicle and riders, objects below about 8~px are not labelled, and a vehicle
that reappears after a full occlusion keeps its identity. Scoring is restricted
to a region of interest drawn on the camera view and frozen before any metric
was computed; a box belongs to it when its bottom centre lies inside. Each
segment was annotated by correcting tracker pre-labels, from C2 for dense and
freeflow and from the baseline for mixed, and then reviewed by one author
through a queue of automatically flagged cases (200, 79 and 54 for dense, mixed
and freeflow; dense had earlier been audited for over-merged identities, which
cut 92 of 105 candidate joins). A blind audit then showed 148 sampled
disagreements between the ground truth and the baseline or C2 output as crops
without tracker or segment names: for each segment and tracker, up to ten
unmatched tracker runs, ten unmatched ground-truth runs and ten identity
switches. The 34 items judged as ground-truth errors or unclear were
re-examined in context in 32 groups, 25 of which led to corrections. The final
ground truth holds 21,749, 9,168 and 2,503 boxes (344, 154 and 48 identities)
for dense, mixed and freeflow.

\subsection{Detector}\label{subsec:detector}

The real-time detector is YOLO11n~\cite{Jocher2024_yolo11} with a detection

trained from the same collection, assembled and annotated by the authors, which
includes frames of traffic cameras other than the evaluation camera. Comparing perceptual hashes of
every image in the version used for the real-time detector (4,980 images) with
frames sampled once per second from all our recordings, and inspecting every
near match, found no image of the evaluation camera; the version behind the
reference was not recorded, so images unique to it are unaudited.
For all
accuracy experiments, detections were computed once per detector and input size
(448, 640, 960 and 1,280~px for the real-time detector, 1,280~px for the
reference) on frames masked outside the region of interest, with a confidence
floor of 0.1, and cached;
every tracker consumes the same cache, so differences between configurations
are attributable to tracking.

\subsection{Configurations, parameters and metrics}\label{subsec:configs}

Exp0 is YOLO11 with unmodified ByteTrack (confirmation threshold 0.50, match
threshold 0.8, second-stage IoU 0.5, a lost-track buffer of 30 frames at 30~fps,
i.e.\ one second at the source's 25~fps); Exp1 adds C2, Exp2 adds C1 and Exp3
adds C3. In every configuration, detections whose bottom centre lies outside the
region of interest are dropped before tracking, and a track whose state leaves
it is removed from both the active and the lost pool (a state whose box becomes
non-finite or inverted counts as having left; this fired 6--34 times per
configuration for BoT-SORT, three times in all for OC-SORT and, with the
reference detector only, four times for our linear filter); C1 measures
brightness inside the region and takes the time of day from the recording. To expose the interaction of the gate and NSA we also run
CTRV alone, CTRV with NSA and CTRV with the gate. As external references,
OC-SORT~\cite{Cao2023_ocsort} and BoT-SORT~\cite{Aharon2022_botsort} run from
their released code on the same detections, without re-identification and,
since the camera is fixed, without camera-motion compensation: once with their
published MOT17 parameters and once with ByteTrack's detection-score thresholds
(association above 0.5, second stage from 0.1, track birth from 0.6; OC-SORT,
which has one threshold for association and birth, runs its optional second
stage with that threshold at 0.5 and at 0.6). The CTRV noise was tuned on
synthetic trajectories and frozen before any real footage was processed; the gate
uses the textbook 95\% value; NSA was adopted on the development frames. Only
C1's \textsc{dense-peak} row was calibrated on the dense segment, which favours
C1. Tracking is scored inside the region of interest with
MOTA~\cite{Bernardin2008_mota},
IDF1~\cite{Ristani2016_idf1}, identity switches (IDSW), fragmentations (Frag),
misses (FN) and false positives (FP) at IoU 0.5, and with
HOTA~\cite{Luiten2021_hota} at the same localization threshold
($\mathrm{HOTA}_{0.5}$; Sect.~\ref{subsec:seedbias} gives the reason), for
motorcycles and over all classes; our implementation is cross-validated against
py-motmetrics~\cite{Heindl_pymotmetrics} and the official HOTA code. C3's discriminative power is
the ROC AUC for separating true from false track-frames.

\subsection{Synthetic sanity check}\label{subsec:synthetic}

On synthetic trajectories with $\sigma=2$~px detection noise and
$v=6$~px/frame, CTRV reduces the one-step prediction RMSE relative to the linear
filter by 48\% on a constant turn (3.43 vs.\ 6.56~px) and by 13\% on sinusoidal
weaving, without regressing on straight motion (3.42 vs.\ 3.59~px). Across a
12-frame occlusion in a turn, CTRV keeps the identity and the linear filter
loses it. The failure boundary is equally clear: when an occlusion begins right
after the turn rate changes sign, CTRV extrapolates the outdated $\omega$. No
noise setting we tried removed this without degrading prediction; it follows
from Proposition~\ref{prop:obs}, since $\omega$ is identified only from second
differences of position.

\subsection{Seed bias in pre-labelled ground truth}\label{subsec:seedbias}

Each ground truth favours the tracker that pre-labelled it. Scored on the
reconciled ground truth, the tracks of our earlier detector give C2 an IDF1
margin over ByteTrack with the sign of the seed: $+0.045$ on dense and $+0.030$
on freeflow (C2-seeded), but $-0.037$ on mixed (baseline-seeded). Box geometry
does not explain it: replacing every ground-truth and predicted box by the
detection it overlaps changes IDF1 and $\mathrm{HOTA}_{0.5}$ by at most 0.002.
HOTA averaged over localization thresholds is the exception (on dense the
margin falls from $+0.124$ to $+0.055$), which is why we report
$\mathrm{HOTA}_{0.5}$. The bias lies in which boxes the ground truth contains.
The reviewer saw every box of the seeding tracker and could delete its errors,
but never saw the boxes that only the other tracker produced, so their absence
is a default rather than a judgement. The blind audit confirms this
(Table~\ref{tab:seedbias}): 8 of 10 sampled runs that the non-seed tracker
reported and the ground truth lacked were real motorcycles, on dense and on
mixed alike, whereas 9 of 10 of its sampled identity switches were real.
Membership is biased; identity switches are not.

\subsection{Context adaptation (C1)}\label{subsec:c1-results}

With its pre-registered thresholds (14, 18, 21 and 23 tracks at 448 to
1,280~px) C1 switches as intended: it holds \textsc{dense-peak} in 80--90\% of
dense frames, in 28--35\% of mixed frames and never in freeflow. It does not
improve tracking. After correction its IDF1 differs from C2's by $-0.003$ to
$-0.002$ on dense and by $+0.001$ to $+0.010$ on mixed, and it adds dense
switches at every size (62 to 100, 95 to 139, 81 to 101 and 103 to 111).
Tracks created during the \textsc{default} stretch that opens the dense segment
(63 to 119 frames, while the smoothed density is still below $\rho^{*}$ and the
hysteresis holds) keep the linear model for life; they carry 14--20\% of the
motorcycle track-frames but take part in 35--44\% of C1's switches at 448 to
960~px (12\% at 1,280~px), so the lifetime rule explains most of C1's switch
cost. On freeflow C1 never leaves \textsc{default}, so its difference there
($+0.019$ to $+0.027$ at 448 to 960~px, $-0.048$ at 1,280~px) is the effect of
that row's 0.45 threshold and linear model, not of adaptation.

\subsection{Track reliability (C3)}\label{subsec:c3-results}

At $\tau_{\mathrm{obs}}=0.40$ C3 filters nothing on any segment, input size or
detector, so Exp3 equals Exp2 frame by frame. The asymmetry that motivates C3
belongs to the accident branch, which we tested at 448~px in a pre-registered
case study. In 1.5~h of normal traffic from six cameras the pipeline raised no
accident alert with or without C3 (95\% upper bound 3.65 per hour on cameras
absent from the detector's training data), so C3's effect on false alerts
cannot be measured. On a 16-s clip of a car hitting a motorcycle it alerted
0.17~s after the onset without C3 and 0.23~s with it; with one positive event
this is a case study, not a recall estimate.

\subsection{Complexity and memory}\label{subsec:complexity}

CTRV prediction and update cost $\mathcal{O}(d^3)$ per motorcycle track with
$d=5$, against $d=8$ for the linear filter, and the Jacobian is closed-form.
Association keeps ByteTrack's $\mathcal{O}(nm)$ cost matrix and its
assignment; the gate adds one $2\times2$ Mahalanobis test per lost motorcycle
track and same-class detection, and NSA one scalar product per update. C1 is
$\mathcal{O}(HW+n)$, dominated by the frame mean, and C3 is $\mathcal{O}(n)$.
Each motorcycle track carries about 1~kB of filter state, so 100 tracks occupy
0.1~MB, negligible next to the detector's weights.

size dominates: at 640~px the detector finds 3.5\% of the small dense
motorcycles that the region-of-interest mask leaves whole. The mask adds a
second loss that appears as resolution recovers recall. A ground-truth box
belongs to the region by its bottom centre, but 34, 44 and 58\% of the
motorcycles under 20~px on dense, mixed and freeflow straddle the region's far
boundary, and the masked detector sees only their lower part: on dense at
1,280~px the real-time detector finds 16\% of these cut motorcycles against
about 30\% of the whole ones, and the reference detector 20\% of the cut ones
with the mask against 76\% without it, while motorcycles of 20~px and taller
barely change (90 against 93\%). The unmasked
detections are the ones that seeded the ground truth, which favours them, and
the mask buys no runtime, because the detector still processes the whole frame.
Rerunning the real-time detector without the mask, all else equal, measures
the mask's net effect on tracking: C2's IDF1 rises on freeflow at every input
size (by 0.06 to 0.18), where most small motorcycles are cut, changes by at most
0.02 on dense and mixed above 448~px, and falls on dense at 960 and 1,280~px
(by 0.007 and 0.022, with more misses). There, without the mask, the detector
scores interior motorcycles slightly lower (median shift about $-0.015$), and
680--830 of them fall below the 0.5 first-stage threshold against 220--260 that
rise above it, so dense misses grow although boundary objects are recovered;
the cause of the shift is not determined.

\subsection{Latency}\label{subsec:latency}

In that sweep the detection head took 26.6~ms per frame end to end at 448~px
(95th percentile 32.9~ms) and 62.9~ms at 640~px (69.6~ms), against the 40~ms
budget of the 25~fps source. A segmentation head of the same scale cost
$2.25\times$ as much at 640~px, which is why the paper run uses a detection
head, and exporting either network to OpenVINO (FP32, FP16) or ONNX Runtime with
default settings was 25--75\% slower than PyTorch. The COCO weights found few
vehicles, so the 448~px run tracked 1.5 objects per frame; substituting the
10~ms that tracking took with the deployed detector (about 38 tracks per frame)
gives about 36~ms; masking leaves the input size unchanged. By these timings
448~px is the real-time operating point, which Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports.

\subsection{Measurement protocol}\label{subsec:protocol-lessons}

Three protocol rules matter more than the effects under study. Timed in one
process, the same baseline measured
13.19~ms per frame when it ran first and 4.07~ms when it ran last, a $3.2\times$
spread that flatters whichever configuration runs last. Warm-up frames must be
discarded (219~ms against 143~ms in steady state). And the processor's
performance state must be checked: every benchmark of the final runs met the
laptop throttled to 32--64\% of nominal performance and was discarded.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
%% =====================================================================

\subsection{Pre-labelled ground truth needs symmetric adjudication}\label{subsec:annotation}

Pre-labelling from a tracker is the economical way to build tracking ground
truth, and our audit shows its price: the seed decided the sign of a
0.04-IDF1 comparison, as large as the effects under study, and confidence
intervals over frames cannot reveal it. Two practices address it. Pre-labels
can come from the union of the trackers to be compared, so that every box any
of them reports reaches the reviewer. Where the seed is fixed, a blind audit of
sampled disagreements estimates the share of real objects among the non-seed
tracker's extras, and the bracket of Sect.~\ref{subsec:seedbias} turns it into
a corrected gap. Identity errors behave differently because the reviewer sees
them: an identity review of dense cut 92 over-merged identities, which
had rewarded gap bridging, and the audited switches were real.

\subsection{Proxy metrics can be optimized into wrong merges}\label{subsec:proxy}

Before ground truth existed, development used proxy metrics on frames
0--8,999. Widening the gate improved every proxy monotonically, while a
teleport check stayed flat, because a wrong re-association is smooth: track A
continues on motorcycle B. The share of bridged gaps whose heading reverses by
more than $90^\circ$ nearly doubled (23.7\% to 44.4\%), revealing wrong merges, and
we kept the textbook gate.

\subsection{Threats to validity}\label{subsec:threats}

\emph{Internal.} Each ground truth was corrected from one tracker's pre-labels
and favours that tracker (Sect.~\ref{subsec:annotation}); the blind audit
sampled at most ten items per stratum, so residual errors remain, and every
review was done by one author. C1's density threshold was fixed by a
pre-registered rule on frames outside the annotated segments, and C2 was not
tuned on any annotated segment.
\emph{External.} All results are development results from the single camera on
which the method was designed: three 24-second segments of one morning with
only cars and motorcycles. No independent camera was annotated, and the
detector's validation images are not an independent test; C3's accident test
rests on one accident clip and on daytime footage. \emph{Construct.}
MOTA, IDF1 and HOTA measure tracking fidelity, not downstream utility for a
signal controller or alert precision. \emph{Real time.} Timings come from one
earlier sweep on one laptop CPU, with COCO weights on the real-time
architecture and before the region-of-interest protocol; the final
configurations were not timed.

%% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
%% =====================================================================

We evaluated a perception pipeline for
motorcycle-dominant mixed traffic on one camera. Its CTRV motion model reduces
motorcycle identity switches in dense traffic against ByteTrack on identical
detections, by 56\% at the 448-px real-time input, 38\% at 640~px and 27--29\%
at 960--1,280~px; the seed-corrected IDF1 gain is at most 0.02 at 448 and
640~px and reaches 0.043 at 1,280~px. Gating alone does not help, nor does C2's
motion model inside OC-SORT; OC-SORT with matched thresholds leads in
seed-corrected IDF1 up to 960~px, and on mixed at every size. C3 filters
nothing at its threshold on these segments; in a one-clip accident case study
its alerting branch keeps the real alert at a two-frame delay, and its effect on
false alerts cannot be measured because none occur. C1, recalibrated, switches
regimes as intended without improving tracking. Two lessons reach beyond this pipeline: ground truth
pre-labelled from one tracker favours it through the boxes it contains, which
union pre-labels or a blind audit of disagreements correct; and on a commodity
CPU the real-time input size and the region-of-interest mask bound accuracy
through small objects. Future work covers an
independent camera annotated from union pre-labels, accident footage for C3's
precision branch, and an
interacting-multiple-model filter~\cite{Blom1988_imm} combining CTRV with a
constant-velocity model.
